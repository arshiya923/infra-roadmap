The [standalone calculator](eigrp-metrics-explorer.html) accepts exactly two authored paths, each with two outgoing links. Bandwidth is a whole number from 1 to 1,000,000,000 kbps; delay is a whole number from zero to 1,000,000 ten-microsecond units. Zero delay is an arithmetic boundary, not proof of target CLI support. Weights remain K1=K3=1 and K2=K4=K5=0. It aggregates minimum bandwidth and cumulative delay, computing the complete current vector and the neighbor's final-link suffix separately. It derives no historical FD, DUAL eligibility, installed route, ECMP or traffic result.

The RFC truncate-first convention computes `256 × (floor(10000000/minimumKbps) + sumDelayUnits)`. The comparison computes `floor(2560000000/minimumKbps) + 256 × sumDelayUnits`. For the same 1544-kbps/2000-unit vector, these yield 2169856 and 2170031, a difference of 175. The comparison follows the pinned FRR bandwidth helper's rounding order but does not emulate its full aggregation, nondefault-coefficient or saturation branches.

For two 100000-kbps links with delay 10 units each, total cost is 30720 and neighbor suffix RD is 28160. Increasing only local delay to 20 yields total 33280 with unchanged suffix RD. Increasing only one bandwidth leaves the other bottleneck intact. Historical FD remains separate. High-speed inverse-zero ties do not prove installed ECMP or doubled throughput.

The source-described wide example is explicitly below 1 Gbps: 100000 kbps and 200 microseconds produce throughput 6553600 plus latency 1310720, total 7864320. With a declared supported RIB scale 128, the example representation is 61440. The calculator does not implement wide metrics. Faster-link latency conversion, mixed neighbors and actual scaling need their own supported context. Changing RIB scale can clear and replace routes; no such command is supplied here.
